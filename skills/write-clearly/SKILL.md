# Write clearly

Lead with what the intended reader needs to understand or do. Use concrete
subjects, active verbs, and one term per concept. Keep exact interface names
and put conditions next to the claims or actions they qualify.

When editing, honor the requested scope. Cut filler and repetition while
preserving the author's meaning, evidence, uncertainty, and exceptions. Let
sentence length follow the thought.

- Make reports and handoffs stand on their own for readers who missed the
  work. Give them the outcome, enough context to understand it, and any
  verification gap that affects their next decision.
- Match the form to the task. Use examples for unfamiliar behavior, tables for
  comparisons, and figures when they make a relationship easier to follow.
  Keep the takeaway and qualifications in prose.
- Review repeated cards, feeds, notifications, or titles as a collection.
  Put the information readers use to distinguish items early and keep
  comparable fields consistent. See [writing repeated items](references/repeated-items.md).

Read the result as the intended reader. Check that they can understand the point
or act without guessing, and that each substantive claim still has the right
support and degree of certainty. See [examples and editing checks](references/examples.md)
for instructions, PR prose, and visual labels.
